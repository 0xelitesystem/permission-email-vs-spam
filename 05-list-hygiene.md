# List hygiene

List hygiene is the practice of keeping your list to engaged, valid addresses by removing dead ones and pruning people who have stopped opening. A clean list lands in inboxes; a bloated one full of dead weight drags your deliverability down for everyone.

## Why hygiene affects deliverability

Mailbox providers watch how recipients engage. A list full of addresses that never open, bounce, or were abandoned signals that your mail is unwanted, and the provider responds by filtering more of it, including to your engaged subscribers. Pruning the dead weight raises your overall engagement rate, which is one of the strongest signals that keeps you in the inbox.

## Handle bounces

A hard bounce means the address is invalid or no longer exists. Remove hard-bouncing addresses promptly, because continuing to mail them tells providers you are not maintaining your list. Soft bounces are temporary, such as a full mailbox, and can be retried, but repeated soft bounces eventually warrant removal too. Letting bounces accumulate is one of the clearest ways to look like a spammer.

## Re-engage, then let go

Subscribers who have not opened in a long while are dead weight that lowers your engagement signals. A re-engagement message can ask whether they still want to hear from you, giving them a clear chance to stay. Those who do not respond should be removed. Holding onto unengaged addresses to keep the subscriber count high trades a vanity number for worse deliverability to everyone else.

## Watch complaints

When recipients mark your mail as spam, that is a complaint, and providers weigh it heavily. A rising complaint rate is a signal that your content, frequency, or list source has a problem. Keep complaints low by mailing only people who opted in, sending what you promised, and making unsubscribing easy so people leave cleanly instead of complaining.

## Quality over count

The temptation is to measure a list by its size. The measure that matters is how many people actually want and act on your mail. A smaller, engaged, clean list outperforms a larger neglected one on deliverability and on results. Regular hygiene is how you keep the list aligned with that reality instead of inflating a number that works against you.
