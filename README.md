# Permission Email vs Spam

A plain-language reference on the difference between permission-based email and spam, why permission is the whole game for email that works, what legally counts as spam, and how to build and maintain a list honestly. Written for solo operators, creators, and small businesses who want email to be an asset rather than a liability.

**Not legal advice.** Email laws differ by country and change over time. This explains the common principles and points you to the questions worth asking, but it does not replace advice from a qualified professional for your jurisdiction and situation.

## The core idea

Permission is the line between email that builds a business and email that damages it. A permission-based list is people who asked to hear from you and expect your mail. Spam is mail sent to people who did not ask, no matter how good the offer. The first compounds in value over time. The second burns trust, harms your sending reputation, and in many places breaks the law.

## The stance this reference takes

Cold email to people who never asked is treated here as a poor foundation, both because it tends not to work and because it carries legal and reputational risk. The alternative, a list of people who opted in, is slower to build and far more durable. This reference is built around that choice.
