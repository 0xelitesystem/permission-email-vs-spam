# Building a list honestly

Grow your list by offering something people want in exchange for their address, making the opt-in clear, and setting honest expectations about what they will receive. An honest list grows slower and performs far better than any shortcut.

## Make the trade clear

People give their email when they get something worth more than the address: a useful resource, ongoing value, early access, a genuine reason to stay in touch. State plainly what they are signing up for and what you will send. When the trade is clear and fair, the people who join are the people who want your mail, which is exactly who you want.

## Get real consent

Use a sign-up that the person actively chooses, rather than pre-checked boxes or addresses collected for one purpose and used for another. Where confirmed opt-in is used, the subscriber clicks a link in a confirmation email to verify the address and the intent. This adds a step, but it produces a cleaner list of real, willing subscribers and protects you from bad addresses and later disputes.

## Set expectations and keep them

Tell people roughly how often you will email and about what, then deliver on that. If you said a weekly note about one topic, a sudden daily blast about something else breaks the deal and drives unsubscribes and complaints. Consistency with the promise you made at signup is what keeps the list engaged and trusting.

## Never buy, rent, or scrape

A list is valuable precisely because the people on it chose to be there. Buying, renting, or scraping addresses fills the list with people who did not, which raises complaints, lowers engagement, and harms deliverability for everyone you mail. There is no version of a purchased list that performs like an earned one. Build it yourself, one real subscriber at a time.

## Let people leave easily

A visible, working unsubscribe is not a leak in the bucket; it is what keeps the list healthy. People who no longer want your mail and cannot easily leave will mark it as spam instead, which is far worse for you than a clean unsubscribe. Make leaving simple, honor it immediately, and treat it as a normal, healthy part of list life.
