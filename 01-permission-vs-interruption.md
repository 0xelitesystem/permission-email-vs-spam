# Permission vs interruption

Permission-based email goes to people who asked to hear from you. Interruption email, including cold outreach and bought or scraped lists, goes to people who did not. The difference is not just etiquette; it determines whether your email works at all.

## Why permission compounds

When someone opts in, they have raised their hand. They expect your mail, recognize your name, and are more likely to open, read, and act. Each message builds on the last, and the list grows more valuable over time as the relationship deepens. A permission list is an asset you own and can rely on.

## Why interruption decays

Mail sent to people who did not ask starts from suspicion. Most of it is ignored, much of it is marked as spam, and the senders who do it repeatedly see their ability to reach anyone decline. The list does not appreciate; it generates complaints. Even when a cold send produces a few replies, the damage to sending reputation and brand can outweigh them.

## Bought and scraped lists

Buying a list or scraping addresses feels like a shortcut to scale, but it inverts the economics. These contacts never asked for you, so complaint rates are high, engagement is low, and mailbox providers learn to distrust your mail. A purchased list can poison the deliverability of the messages you send to people who did opt in. The shortcut tends to cost more than it saves.

## The slower path is the real one

Building a permission list is slower because each subscriber chooses to join. That slowness is the point: it filters for people who actually want what you send. A smaller list of genuinely interested people outperforms a large list of strangers on nearly every measure that matters, including the only one that pays, which is action taken.

## What this means in practice

Treat the question who asked for this as the first filter on any email you plan to send. If the answer is no one, the right move is not a better subject line; it is to build the list properly so that next time the answer is these people did.
