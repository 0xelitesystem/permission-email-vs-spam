# Email law basics

Most commercial email laws share a common set of requirements: tell the truth about who you are and what the message is, give a clear way to opt out, honor opt-outs promptly, and identify the message appropriately. The details, names, and penalties differ by country, so treat this as orientation, not legal advice.

## The common principles

Across jurisdictions, commercial email rules tend to require several things. Do not use false or misleading header information, so the from, to, and routing reflect who actually sent it. Do not use deceptive subject lines that misrepresent the content. Identify the message as a commercial message where required. Include a valid physical postal address for the sender. Provide a clear and working way to opt out. And honor opt-out requests promptly, after which you stop mailing that person.

## Opt-in vs opt-out regimes

Jurisdictions differ in how much consent they require before the first message. Some operate closer to an opt-out model for certain commercial mail, where you may send but must offer a way out. Others require opt-in consent before you send at all, with stricter rules and larger penalties. If you mail people in multiple countries, the strictest applicable rule effectively sets your standard. Confirm which regimes apply to your audience.

## Penalties are real

Violations can carry significant per-message penalties in some jurisdictions, and the liability can extend to the business on whose behalf the mail was sent, not only the sender. The financial exposure is one more reason the permission-first approach is the safe one: it keeps you comfortably inside the rules everywhere.

## Why permission keeps you compliant

If you only mail people who opted in, are honest about who you are and what you are sending, and make unsubscribing easy and immediate, you satisfy the spirit of nearly every email law at once. Permission is not just good practice; it is the simplest way to stay compliant across the patchwork of rules.

## Confirm for your situation

Because the specifics depend on where you and your recipients are, and because the rules change, the responsible move for anything that carries legal weight is to confirm with a qualified professional. This reference is meant to help you understand the shape of the rules and ask the right questions, not to substitute for that advice.
